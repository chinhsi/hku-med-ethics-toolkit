# English wording examples for a student researcher

Adapted from the upstream MIT-licensed hku-consent-form language bank. These phrases are examples, not official approval or study facts. Current MEd rules take priority. Do not supply missing procedures, risk, retention or contacts from these examples.

## Researcher and audience
Adult/teacher: “I am a student in [programme] at the Faculty of Education, The University of Hong Kong, supervised by [name]. I invite you to consider the study described below.”
Principal: “I ask for permission for the school activities described below. School permission does not replace each participant's consent or a child's assent.”
Parent: “We invite your child to take part in the activities below. Your child may choose not to join.”
Child: “You can say no. You can ask us to stop. Your teacher will not be upset with you.” Use this last sentence only when supported by concrete safeguards and appropriate to the actual relationship.

## Voluntary participation and recording
“Taking part is voluntary. You may skip a question or stop. [Explain actual consequences/safeguards and data-withdrawal method/cut-off.]”
“Recording will begin only if you agree. [Explain what happens if recording is declined.] You may review the recording and request deletion of part or all of it while it is retained. [Explain the actual request method and timing.]”

## Confidentiality
“Reports will use pseudonyms and remove or generalise identifying details. The researcher will know who you are, so the interview is confidential rather than anonymous at collection. [State access, storage, key management and retention.] Confidentiality may be limited by applicable legal disclosure obligations. [State relevant additional safeguards.]”

## Contacts
“Student researcher: [name, programme, email]. Supervisor: [name, email, readily reachable supervisor telephone for Hong Kong studies]. Participant rights: Human Research Ethics Committee, HKU, +852 3917 5267, hrec@hku.hk.”

Use blank ethics reference fields in drafts; use the real authority and reference from the actual approval letter before recruitment. Never invent a number or sign a declaration.
